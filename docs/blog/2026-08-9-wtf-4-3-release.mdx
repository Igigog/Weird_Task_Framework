## Quests

Ecologists are eating good with this release. WTF brings two new quests for Ecologists, reworks an older Mercenary quest and changes the way _Realistic Assassinations_ work:

- [_Onboarding_](/tasks/community-tasks/detector_upgrade_easy) is a single-time quest for new Ecologists, where you learn the routine of Ecologists by measuring an anomaly around Yantar. Aside from normal rewards, Sakharov takes a pity on you and swaps your Echo detector with a Bear detector - as expected from their faction, Ecologists get an easier way to start collecting artifacts early on.
- [_Big Measuring Contest_](/tasks/community-tasks/detector_upgrade_hard) plays with the formula of _Onboarding_, but is intended for late-game Ecologist runs. When you've proven yourself a professional, Hermann in Jupiter asks you to measure all the anomalies on the whole Jupiter map. For that feat, alongside conventional rewards, Hermann improves your Veles detector into Svarog.

- [_Between Law And Profit_](/tasks/community-tasks/medic_package) got a rework. Now, you have finite time to meet your contact, and if you don't manage it in time, you'll find the package stolen and your contact dead.

### Arszi's Realistic Assassinations

WTF 4.3 marks the removal of _Arszi's Realistic Assassinations_ option. Now, instead of your quest failing randomly when someone else kills your target, you are still able to complete the quest. There is a twist, though: you're not a psychic and you can't know if your target is dead. As such, you still need to go to the location and confirm the dead enemies with your own eyes.

I think this mechanic works both narratively and from game design perspective. It is now always enabled in GT Pack's [_Neutralization_](/tasks/gt-tasks/heavy) and [_Eliminate dangerous mutants_](/tasks/gt-tasks/mutant) quests.

### Rewards

WTF 4.3 rebalances and changes the logic of quest rewards. 

- The rewards were brought in line with vanilla Anomaly; I tried my best to keep original game's reward balance.
- WTF now respects the game's progression settings in the rewards.
- Global reward modifiers were removed from WTF's MCM menu, though you can still modify rewards of single tasks.
- Reward definitions were greatly simplified in backend, making them easier to understand for both players and questmakers

### Descriptions

- Level names were added to location descriptions in RUS localization.
- Dynamic descriptions of some quests were cleaned up to keep the objective clearer.
- Quest rewards are not shown in PDA description anymore. They weren't always correct anyway - fetch weapon quests where reward depends on condition can't really show static reward. Rewards are still shown in the task dialogue.
- Dynamic descriptions are now set explicitly, giving quest makers more control over what's show and what's not.

## Frames

The biggest improvement in WTF 4.3 is the experimental support for multi-stage quests. They work using _Frames_ - essentially, every quest stage is its own sub-quest. This abstraction proved to be expressive and easy to work with, though somewhat boilerplate-y. I am sure that frames will be used extensively in future WTF quests, though I am yet working out all the smaller implementation details.

## Future of WTF

When I started WTF, I didn't expect to make a whole-ass task pack of my own, with quests more diverse and polished than both GhenTuong and Arszi's packs WTF was born out of. There are more questmakers in the community now than there were in the days WTF was born; quests and questlines are more diverse as ever, and I celebrate that. It's just nice, you know? I love quests, that's why I do that. Anyway, here's my thoughts on where I want to bring WTF next.

### Awkward Scenario Engine

Once you have a hammer, everything kinda looks like a nail, right? WTF is a task framework, but over time I started seeing potential for more. The same systems that power quest stages can be reused for cutscenes, or randomized game events, or just literally any actions in the game. In the end, what is a quest if not a cutscene with some player input and a task marker?

I want to separate WTF from task manager and use it more generically for any types of game events. It is a task framework now, but I want to evolve it into a scenario engine; tasks will be just a special case of scenarios. I don't know yet what exactly I want to do with that newfound power, but... It's about the journey too, you know? ;)

### Anomaly ReQuesting Project

Current quests in WTF task packs are just a dump of random dynamic tasks. They are very much missing any common direction or guidelines; they were born independently. I think it is time to change that.

The direction of Community Task Pack showed that I like creating small unique quests. [_Orders!_](/tasks/community-tasks/orders_army) series, [_Between Law and Profit_](/tasks/community-tasks/medic_package), [_The More You Know_](/tasks/community-tasks/sid_bribery) and both new Ecologist quests, [_Onboarding_](/tasks/community-tasks/detector_upgrade_easy) and [_Big Measuring Contest_](/tasks/community-tasks/detector_upgrade_hard) are small quests unique to a single task giver, sometimes available to only a single faction. I love all of them. I want more of this. And I also can make more of this - every single one of these quests was created in one evening, about 3-5 hours total per quest.

So, I am starting a project to replace all of Anomaly's dynamic quests with ones of my own, deeper and more polished, with a single coherent direction. What I want to achieve is:

- Unique quests for every faction. Playthrough for a different faction should _feel_ different, _progress_ different, and have unique mechanics.
- One faction per release. I'm starting with Ecologists, since I already have 3 quests for them.
- 20-40 quests per faction. All of them should represent the faction in some way, and at least half of them should only be accessible to players of the faction.

I haven't decided my course of actions yet. You might help me.
